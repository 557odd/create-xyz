📐 The Mathematical Blueprint

To make this a bulletproof geometric puzzle, we can use exact coordinate geometry, vectors, and timestamps. The core mechanism uses a geometric intersection to prove that a physical obstruction blocks one observer's line of sight during a critical window of time.
Below is the precise grid setup, the conflicting testimonies, and the mathematical proof the players must solve.

• The Obstruction (A Thick Wall): A straight security wall runs along the line segment from \((1, 2)\) to \((4, 2)\).

📜 The Witness Statements

The player receives three logs recorded at 15:00 (3:00 PM):
• Observer A Log (at \(1, 0\)):
	• Time: "At exactly 14:00, I looked due North-West toward the Target."
	• Visual Clue: "The Target was displaying a Triangle symbol."
• Observer B Log (at \(4, 5\)):
	• Time: "At exactly 14:00, I looked South-West toward the Target."
	• Visual Clue: "Observer A is mistaken. I saw the Target at that exact moment, and it was displaying a Square symbol."
• Observer C Log (at \(5, 0\)):
	• Time: "I monitored the area between 13:55 and 14:05."
	• Visual Clue: "I had a clear line of sight to Observer A. At 14:00, Observer A was indeed looking at the Target. I also know for a fact that the Target was displaying a Triangle at 14:00, because the symbol only changes to a Square at 15:00."

🔍 The Geometric Proof (How the Player Solves It)

To find the liar, the player must determine which observer's line of sight is physically impossible due to the wall.

1. Testing Observer A's Sightline

• Line starts at \((1, 0)\) and goes to Target \((2, 4)\).
• The equation of this line is: \(y = 4x - 4\).
• Where it crosses the wall's horizontal plane (\(y = 2\)): \(2 = 4x - 4 \implies 6 = 4x \implies \mathbf{x = 1.5}\).
• Since the wall exists between \(x = 1\) and \(x = 4\) at \(y = 2\), the intersection point \((1.5, 2)\) means Observer A's view is blocked by the wall. Observer A could not have seen the Triangle at 14:00.

2. Testing Observer B's Sightline

• Line starts at \((4, 5)\) and goes to Target \((2, 4)\).
• The equation of this line is: \(y = 0.5x + 3\).
• Where it crosses the wall's horizontal plane (\(y = 2\)): \(2 = 0.5x + 3 \implies -1 = 0.5x \implies \mathbf{x = -2}\).
• Because \(x = -2\) falls completely outside the wall's physical boundaries (\(x = 1\) to \(4\)), Observer B has a totally clear line of sight.

3. The Climax (Exposing the Liar)

Observer B saw a Square, but Observer C's chronological data proves the Target was a Triangle at 14:00. If Observer A's view was blocked, Observer A must have been guessing or repeating old information—but Observer B is actively lying because they had a perfect view of a Triangle, yet fabricated seeing a Square to make Observer A look wrong.
